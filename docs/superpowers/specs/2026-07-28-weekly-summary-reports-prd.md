# SADC PF Nexus

# Weekly Summary Reports Module

## Full Updated Product Requirements Document

**System:** SADC PF Nexus Internal Paperless Administration System
**Module:** Weekly Summary Reports
**Short name:** Weekly Summaries
**Document status:** Updated implementation PRD
**Module type:** Individual, departmental and institutional operational-progress reporting module

The next module is **Weekly Summary Reports**.

This module must consolidate work already captured in Assignments, Timesheets, PIF, M&E, Correspondence, Travel, Procurement and Risk rather than forcing employees to type the same information again.

The Accounting Manual requires programme and project progress information to support analysis of direct operational expenditure and requires timely donor feedback under applicable grant arrangements.   The Weekly Summary Module should support these broader reporting responsibilities while remaining an internal operational report—not a replacement for M&E, donor reports, financial reports or formal performance appraisal.

---

# 1. Executive Summary

The Weekly Summary Reports Module will provide SADC PF with a structured system for employees, departments and Management to report:

* work completed during the week;
* work still in progress;
* assignments and activities supported;
* notable outputs and deliverables;
* meetings and missions attended;
* decisions made;
* blockers and constraints;
* matters requiring Management intervention;
* risks identified;
* overdue or delayed work;
* priorities for the coming week;
* deadlines requiring attention;
* support required from other departments;
* linked evidence and source records.

The module will produce four principal report levels:

1. **Individual Weekly Summary**
2. **Departmental Weekly Summary**
3. **Directorate or Functional Summary**
4. **Institutional Management Weekly Summary**

The central product rule is:

> Weekly summaries must transform verified operational data into concise management information without duplicating source records or turning activity counts into automated employee-performance scores.

---

# 2. Product Principle

The module must answer:

* What meaningful work was completed this week?
* What remains in progress?
* Which assignments, activities and institutional obligations were supported?
* What deliverables were produced?
* What deadlines are approaching?
* What is delayed?
* Why is it delayed?
* What decisions are required?
* Which matters require cross-department support?
* What risks or emerging issues need escalation?
* What are the priorities for next week?
* What should Management know now?
* Which information came from an authoritative Nexus record?
* Which information was entered manually by the employee?
* Has the supervisor reviewed the summary?
* Has the departmental summary been consolidated?
* Has Management acted on requests for decision or support?

# 4. What the Module Is Not

The module is not:

* a second Timesheet;
* a duplicate Assignment Register;
* an M&E report;
* a donor report;
* a financial report;
* a PIF completion report;
* a performance appraisal;
* a disciplinary record;
* an employee surveillance tool;
* a list of every email sent;
* a place to copy every minor action completed during the week.

The focus must be:

> Meaningful progress, important constraints, decisions required and priorities ahead.

# 5. Demo Changes Incorporated

## 5.1 No duplicate re-entry

The demo requirement is that employees should not manually reproduce:

* assignments completed;
* tasks in progress;
* Timesheet activities;
* approved travel;
* PIF activities;
* correspondence actions;
* M&E follow-ups.

Nexus should suggest these records for inclusion.

The employee confirms, edits the narrative context and selects what is relevant.

---

## 5.2 Automatic suggestions, not automatic submission

Nexus may suggest:

* completed Assignments;
* assignments worked on;
* upcoming deadlines;
* approved travel;
* activities attended;
* blocked tasks;
* overdue tasks;
* decisions awaiting action.

However:

* the system must not fabricate narrative;
* it must not automatically submit a report;
* the employee must review and confirm the content;
* confidential records must not be exposed through suggestions.

---

## 5.3 Supervisor and Management visibility

Supervisors must see:

* who submitted;
* who has not submitted;
* key achievements;
* blockers;
* support requested;
* next-week priorities;
* issues requiring escalation.

Management must see a consolidated view rather than opening every individual report.

---

## 5.4 Blockers must be actionable

A blocker should not be a passive paragraph.

Where appropriate, the user must be able to identify:

* blocker;
* blocker owner;
* support required;
* decision required;
* deadline;
* escalation level.

The blocker may create:

* an Assignment;
* a Risk;
* a Management Decision request;
* a cross-department action.

---

## 5.5 Confidentiality

The system must not automatically place confidential assignment titles or restricted correspondence into broadly visible summaries.

Users must receive a warning before including restricted content.

---

## 5.6 Weekly summaries are not performance scores

The system must not automatically rank employees based on:

* number of tasks listed;
* number of hours worked;
* number of assignments completed;
* length of weekly report;
* number of meetings attended.

Quality and institutional contribution require human judgment and context.

---

# 6. Business Objectives

The module must:

1. Give Management timely operational visibility.
2. Reduce time spent producing weekly reports.
3. Eliminate duplicate reporting.
4. Provide consistent report structure.
5. Surface blockers before they become major delays.
6. Support interdepartmental coordination.
7. Preserve a weekly institutional record.
8. Track priorities from one week to the next.
9. Connect achievements to authoritative Nexus records.
10. Highlight decisions requiring Management action.
11. Support donor/project and programme reporting preparation.
12. Improve accountability without creating intrusive surveillance.
13. Support departmental and institutional consolidation.
14. Provide dashboards and exports.
15. Preserve review, correction and submission history.

Less useful:

> Attended meeting.

> Participated in the planning meeting and completed the technical requirements list for the Plenary accreditation system.

# 35. Data Inclusion Control

Every source suggestion must show:

* source module;
* source reference;
* source status;
* visibility;
* include/exclude checkbox;
* editable narrative field.

Source metadata should remain read-only.

# 36. Source Snapshot

When a source-linked item is submitted in a weekly report, preserve a limited snapshot:

* source reference;
* title;
* status;
* date;
* responsible user;
* source module.

This allows historical reports to remain understandable even when the source later changes.

The live link should also remain available.

# 37. Duplicate Detection

The system should detect when the same source item is being reported under:

* achievement;
* work in progress;
* blocker;
* next-week priority.

It may warn but should allow legitimate multi-section use where the context differs.

# 52. Duplicate Consolidation

If several employees report the same event, the Department Summary should consolidate them into one item.

Example:

Three employees attended the same activity.

# 73. Versioning

Every submission and correction must create a version.

Example:

* Draft
* Submitted v1
* Returned
* Resubmitted v2
* Accepted v2

Department consolidations and Management summaries also require version history.

---

# 74. Post-Acceptance Correction

An accepted report should not be silently edited.

Correction process:

1. Reopen requested.
2. Reason recorded.
3. Authorised user approves.
4. New version created.
5. Consolidated reports impacted are flagged.
6. Revised report resubmitted/reviewed.

---

# 75. Consolidation Freeze

Once a department or institutional summary is published:

* source report corrections do not silently rewrite it;
* affected summary is marked `Source Updated`;
* authorised consolidator decides whether to issue a revised summary.

Historical published versions remain preserved.

Where formal delegation allows submission, the action must be clearly attributed.

# 80. Donor Reporting Boundary

Weekly summaries may help prepare donor reports, but they must not automatically become official donor submissions.

Official donor reports require:

* applicable donor format;
* programme validation;

* Finance data;
* approval;

The Accounting Manual requires timely donor feedback and donor-specific reporting arrangements, but the Weekly Summary is supporting evidence rather than the donor report itself.

# 121. Critical Architecture Rules

The implementation team must treat the following as non-negotiable:

> **Weekly Summaries must reuse source data rather than require duplicate entry.**

> **Source modules remain authoritative.**

> **Suggestions are not submissions.**

> **The employee must confirm the final individual report.**

> **A Department Summary must consolidate, not merely concatenate.**

> **Original employee reports must not be silently rewritten by a supervisor or consolidator.**

> **Blockers and decisions required must be structured and actionable.**

> **Repeatedly carried priorities must remain historically traceable.**

> **Confidential source information must not leak into broader reports.**

> **Weekly reports are not Timesheets, M&E reports or performance appraisals.**

> **The system must not rank employees by report length, task count or recorded hours.**

> **Management decisions must create traceable follow-up actions where required.**

> **Published reports are versioned institutional records and must not be silently changed.**
